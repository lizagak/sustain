I am a Postdoctoral Fellow of Co-design of Technology with Children at the Joan Ganz Cooney Center at Sesame Workshop. I completed my PhD in Human-Computer Interaction at the School of Information at UC Berkeley in May 2025, where I was advised by Dr. Niloufar Salehi.

My research focuses on how teens and young adults engage with sociotechnical systems in everyday life—particularly around online harms, privacy, interpersonal surveillance, and algorithmic injustice. I am inspired by applying creative and action-based methods like Youth Participatory Action Research (YPAR), speculative design, co-design, interviews, and design probes to support youth as co-researchers, storytellers, and action-takers.

I’ve participated and published in academic research communities across CHI, CSCW, and IDC

.

My research has been generously supported by the National Science Foundation Graduate Research Fellowship, the Center for Technology, Society, and Policy, and the Algorithmic Fairness and Opacity Group. At Berkeley, I had the pleasure of teaching INFO 188: Behind the Data – Humans and Values with Prof. Deirdre Mulligan, as well as INFO 203: Social Issues of Information with Dr. Morgan Ames.

I graduated from Washington University in St. Louis, where I studied Computer Science, Mathematics, and American Culture Studies.
